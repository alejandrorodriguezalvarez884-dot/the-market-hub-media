Today at 1:50 PM UTC Grace O'Donnell PCE annual inflation rises to 3.8% in October Inflation rose last month, likely cementing a Fed rate hike in December. The Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) index rose 0.3% month over month in October and 3.8% year over year, the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) reported on Wednesday. The annual reading was the highest since 2023. On a "core" basis — stripping out volatile food and energy prices — the PCE index increased 0.3% month over month and 3.3% year over year, a tick higher than economists' expectations for a 3.2% annual increase. Traders priced in a roughly 98% chance that the Fed raises interest rates by 25 basis points at its meeting next month, per CME Group. Today at 1:38 PM UTC Grace O'Donnell GDP accelerates to 4.3% in Q3, driven by investments and consumer spending Economic activity picked up in the third quarter, a sign of strong growth for the US. The final reading of third quarter gross domestic product (GDP) increased at an annual rate of 4.3%, the Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) reported on Wednesday, picking up the pace from 2.2% growth in Q2. The final read was also ahead of the advance estimate of Q3 GDP of 3.4% and well ahead of economists' estimates of 3.5%. BEA said that the growth was driven by increases in investment, consumer spending, and government spending, which outweighed a decrease in exports. Imports, which are subtracted in the calculation of GDP, increased. Today at 1:19 PM UTC Ben Werschkul US announces new tariffs of up to 20% on China, the EU, and 12 other nations The Trump administration proposed new tariffs of up to 20% on China, the European Union, and 12 other major US trading partners to take effect next year, following an 8-month investigation into excess manufacturing capacity. The duties could be a shock to the global economy, with China a main target. The proposed 20% tariff on many Chinese goods would be stacked on top of at least some preexisting tariffs, for a total rate over 40% in some areas. The new tariffs won't go into effect until after additional hearings and a public comment period and "probably will be in January at the earliest," according to a US official who briefed reporters ahead of the announcement. Today at 1:00 PM UTC Grace O'Donnell 10 big money topics you might have to tackle over turkey Yahoo Finance's Hamza Shaban and David Hollerith put together a little cheat sheet of 10 big topics from the world of finance to guide you through your Thanksgiving dinner conversation. Here's their guide to the first topic: the stock market. The main takeaway for the stock market these days is "wow." We have had an extremely zesty month , from the midterm elections pouring water on companies building data centers and raising hopes the Iran war will end to the Anthropic IPO to the Nvidia… drama (more on that below). Remember: The stock market is still up over 5% for the year. Not a great return, historically, but it's still positive. The bullish take: With stocks down (or somewhat down), this is a great buying opportunity — just look at the favorable price-to-earnings ratio the S&P 500 has! Corporate earnings are the backbone of any valuation, and companies are earning well and expect that to continue. The consumer is still strong, stocks usually do well after a period of fear, and as the TKer's Sam Ro likes to remind us, "stocks usually go up over the long term." The bearish take: The stock market's foundation may be strong consumer spending, but its big gains are largely AI-driven. Have you seen how concentrated the S&P 500 is in just a few Big Tech companies? If the AI trade can't pay for itself, those massive investments (and degrading chips) will cause some big problems. Plus, do normal people want AI? Will they pay for it? Because at these spending rates, the math looks tough. And the Fed is going to hike rates.
