Topic of the Week
Lowest Forward 12-Month P/E Ratio For the S&P 500 in More Than 3 Years

On November 5, the forward 12-month P/E ratio for the S&P 500 was 17.7. This forward 12-month P/E ratio was based on
a closing price of 7353.73 and a forward 12-month EPS estimate of $415.82. How does this 17.7 P/E ratio compare to
historical averages? What is driving the recent increase in the P/E ratio?

The forward 12-month P/E ratio of 17.7 on November 5 was below the 5-year average of 19.8 and below the 10-year
average of 19.1. In fact, this marked the lowest forward 12-month P/E ratio recorded bv the index since November 1, 2023
(17.5).

However, it is important to note that this forward 12-month P/E ratio was still above the 15-year (17.6), 20-year (16.5), and
25-year (16.4) averages. It is also well above the lowest forward 12-month P/E ratio recorded by the index over the past
25 years of 8.6 on November 20, 2008.

At the sector level, six sectors had forward 12-month P/E ratios on November 5, 2026, that were below their 5-year
averages and six sectors also has forward 12-month P/E ratios that were below their 10-year averages. However, only two
sectors had forward 12-month P/E ratios on that date that were below their 25-year averages: Information Technology
(17.3 vs. 20.0) and Energy (11.7 vs. 13.9). For the Information Technology sector, this marked the lowest forward 12-
month P/E ratio recorded by the sector since March 23, 2020 (16.1).

What is driving the decline in the forward 12-month P/E ratio for the S&P 500? At the start of the year (December 31,
2025), the forward 12-month P/E ratio for the S&P 500 was 22.0. Since the start of the year, the price of the S&P 500 has
increased by 7.4%, while the forward 12-month EPS estimate increased by 33.6%. Thus, the increase in the “E” has been
the main driver of the decrease in the P/E ratio since the start of the year.

Q3 Earnings Season: By The Numbers
Overview
At the end of the peak weeks of the earnings season, the S&P 500 is reporting mixed results for Q3 relative to expectations.
While the percentage of S&P 500 companies reporting positive earnings surprises is above recent averages, the magnitude
of earnings surprises is below recent averages. As a result, the index is reporting higher earnings for the third quarter today
relative to the end of last week and relative to the end of the quarter. In addition, the index is reporting (year-over-year)
earnings growth above 25% for the 3rd straight quarter.

Overall, 88% of the companies in the S&P 500 have reported actual results for Q3 2026 to date. Of these companies, 84%
have reported actual EPS above estimates, which is above the 5-year average of 78% and above the 10-year average of
77%. In aggregate, companies are reporting earnings that are 2.7% above estimates, which is below the 5-year average
of 7.5% and below the 10-year average of 8.0%. If 2.7% is the actual number for the quarter, it will mark the lowest earnings
surprise percentage reported by the index since Q4 2022 (1.3%). Historical averages reflect actual results from all 500
companies, not the actual results from the percentage of companies that have reported through this point in time.

During the past week, positive EPS surprises reported by companies in multiple sectors (led by companies in the Health
Care and Information Technology sectors), partially offset by a negative EPS surprise reported by a company in the
Communication Services sector, were the largest contributors to the increase in the overall earnings growth rate for the
index over this period. Since September 30, positive EPS surprises reported by companies in multiple sectors (led by
companies in the Energy and Information Technology sectors), partially offset by negative EPS surprises reported by
companies in the Communication Services sector, have been the largest contributors to the increase in the overall earnings
growth rate for the index over this period.

As a result, the index is reporting higher earnings for the third quarter today relative to the end of last week and relative to
the end of the quarter. The blended (combines actual results for companies that have reported and estimated results for
companies that have yet to report) earnings growth rate for the third quarter is 31.4% today, compared to an earnings
growth rate of 30.1% last week and an earnings growth rate of 29.1% at the end of the third quarter (September 30).

If 31.4% is the actual growth rate for the quarter, it will mark the third consecutive quarter of earnings growth above 25%
and the eighth consecutive quarter of double-digit earnings growth for the index.

All eleven sectors are reporting year-over-year earnings growth. Seven of these sectors are reporting double-digit growth,
led by the Energy, Information Technology, and Materials sectors.

In terms of revenues, 74% of S&P 500 companies have reported actual revenues above estimates, which is above the 5-
year average of 70% and above the 10-year average of 68%. In aggregate, companies are reporting revenues that are
2.1% above the estimates, which is above the 5-year average of 1.8% and above the 10-year average of 1.6%. Again,
historical averages reflect actual results from all 500 companies, not the actual results from the percentage of companies
that have reported through this point in time.

During the past week, positive revenue surprises reported by companies in multiple sectors (led by companies in the
Energy, Health Care, and Information Technology sectors) were the largest contributors to the increase in the overall
revenue growth rate for the index over this period. Since September 30, positive revenue surprises reported by companies
in the Energy, Health Care, and Information Technology sectors have been the largest contributors to the increase in the
overall revenue growth rate for the index over this period.

As a result, the blended revenue growth rate for the third quarter is 14.3% today, compared to a revenue growth rate of
13.6% last week and a revenue growth rate of 12.2% at the end of the third quarter (September 30).

Scorecard: Percentages of Positive EPS & Revenue Surprises Are Above 5-Year Averages

Percentage of Companies Beating EPS Estimates (84%) is Above 5-Year Average
Overall, 88% of the companies in the S&P 500 have reported earnings to date for the third quarter. Of these companies,
84% have reported actual EPS above the mean EPS estimate, 4% have reported actual EPS equal to the mean EPS
estimate, and 12% have reported actual EPS below the mean EPS estimate. The percentage of companies reporting EPS
above the mean EPS estimate is above the 1-year average (82%), above the 5-year average (78%), and above the 10-
year average (77%). Historical averages reflect actual results from all 500 companies, not the actual results from the
percentage of companies that have reported through this point in time.

At the sector level, the Energy (95%), Information Technology (94%), and Health Care (93%) sectors have the highest
percentages of companies reporting earnings above estimates, while the Materials (57%) sector have the lowest
percentage of companies reporting earnings above estimates.

Earnings Surprise Percentage (+2.7%) is Below 5-Year Average
In aggregate, companies are reporting earnings that are 2.7% above expectations. This surprise percentage is below the
1-year average (+13.8%), below the 5-year average (+7.5%), and below the 10-year average (+8.0%). Historical averages
reflect actual results from all 500 companies, not the actual results from the percentage of companies that have reported
through this point in time.

If 2.7% is the actual number for the quarter, it will mark the lowest earnings surprise percentage reported by the index
since Q4 2022 (+1.3%).

The Health Care (+13.7%) sector is reporting the largest positive (aggregate) difference between actual earnings and
estimated earnings. Within this sector, Centene Corporation ($0.58 vs. $0.10), Moderna ($0.38 vs. -$1.10), Elevance
Health ($6.99 vs. $4.71), Biogen ($2.62 vs. $1.99), Humana (-0.70 vs. -1.00), and Eli Lilly and Company ($7.48 vs. $5.71)
have reported the largest positive EPS surprises. The (non-GAAP) EPS actual for Elevance Health for Q3 2026 included
a net non-operating benefit of $1.85.

The Utilities (+8.2%) sector (along with the Energy sector) is reporting the second-largest positive (aggregate) difference
between actual earnings and estimated earnings. Within this sector, AES Corporation ($0.86 vs. $0.53) and WEC Energy
Group ($1.18 vs. $0.97) have reported the largest positive EPS surprises.

Market Punishing Negative EPS Surprises Less Than Average
To date, the market is rewarding positive earnings surprises reported by S&P 500 companies for Q3 slightly less than
average and also punishing negative earnings surprises reported by S&P 500 companies for Q3 less than average.

Companies that have reported positive earnings surprises for Q3 2026 have seen an average change in price of +0.8%
two days before the earnings release through two days after the earnings release. This percentage increase is slightly
below the 5-year average price increase of +0.9% during this same window for companies reporting positive earnings
surprises.

Companies that have reported negative earnings surprises for Q3 2026 have seen an average change in price of -1.1%
two days before the earnings release through two days after the earnings release. This percentage decrease is smaller
than the 5-year average price decrease of -3.1% during this same window for companies reporting negative earnings
surprises.

in Communication Services sectors, were the largest contributors to the increase in the overall earnings growth rate for the
index over this period.
