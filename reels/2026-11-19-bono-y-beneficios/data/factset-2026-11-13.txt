Topic of the Week: 1
Market Is Punishing Negative EPS Surprises Less Than Average for S&P 500 Companies for Q3

To date, 91% of the companies in the S&P 500 have reported earnings for the third quarter. Of these companies, 84%
have reported actual EPS above the mean EPS estimate, which is above the 5-year average of 78% and above the 10-
year average of 77%. In aggregate, companies are reporting earnings that are 3.1% above estimates, which is below the
5-year average of 7.5% and below the 10-year average of 8.0%. If 3.1% is the actual number for the quarter, it will mark
the lowest surprise percentage reported by the index since Q4 2022 (1.3%). Please see pages 7 to 8 for more details.

Given this mixed performance relative to recent averages, how has the market responded to EPS surprises reported by
S&P 500 companies during the Q3 earnings season?

To date, the market is rewarding positive earnings surprises reported by S&P 500 companies for the third quarter more
than average.

Companies that have reported positive earnings surprises for Q3 2026 have seen an average price increase of 1.1% two
days before the earnings release through two days after the earnings release. This percentage increase is above the 5-
year average price increase of 0.9% during this same window for companies reporting positive earnings surprises.

On the other hand, the market is punishing negative earnings surprises reported by S&P 500 companies for the third
quarter less than average.

Companies that have reported negative earnings surprises for Q3 2026 have seen an average price decrease of 1.1% two
days before the earnings release through two days after the earnings. This percentage decrease is much smaller than the
5-year average price decrease of 3.1% during this same window for companies reporting negative earnings surprises.

What is driving the market’s more positive and less negative reactions to EPS surprises for Q3? It may be due to positive
revisions to EPS estimates for Q4 by analysts and positive EPS guidance for Q4 issued by companies to date.

In terms of revisions to EPS estimates for S&P 500 companies, analysts have increased EPS estimates for Q4 2026 to
date. The Q4 bottom-up EPS estimate (which is an aggregation of the median EPS estimates for Q4 for all the companies
in the index) has increased by 1.1% (to $94.49 from $93.49) since September 30. In a typical quarter, analysts usually
reduce earnings estimates during the quarter.

In terms of EPS guidance, the percentage of S&P 500 companies issuing positive EPS guidance for Q4 is above average.
At this point in time, 78 companies in the index have issued EPS guidance for Q4 2026. Of these 78 companies, 45 have
issued positive EPS guidance and 33 have issued negative EPS guidance. The percentage of companies issuing positive
EPS guidance for Q4 2026 is 58% (45 out of 78), which is above the 5-year average of 40% and above the 10-year average
of 41%.

It is interesting to note that the market has punished EPS surprises reported by the “Magnificent 7” companies for Q3 more
than the entire S&P 500.

The three “Magnificent 7” companies that reported positive EPS surprises for Q3 2026 (Apple, Microsoft, and Tesla) saw
an average price decrease of 0.2% two days before the earnings release through two days after the earnings release. This
compares to an average price increase of 1.1% for all S&P 500 companies reporting positive EPS surprises for Q3.

The three “Magnificent 7” companies that reported negative EPS surprises for Q3 2026 (Alphabet, Amazon.com, and Meta
Platforms) saw an average price decrease of 7.8% two days before the earnings release through two days after the
earnings release. This compares to an average price decrease of 1.1% for all S&P 500 companies reporting negative EPS
surprises for Q3.

Topic of the Week: 2
S&P 500 Companies Are Reporting Lowest Earnings Surprise Percentage Since Q4 2022

At this late stage of the Q3 earnings season, aggregate actual earnings reported by S&P 500 companies have exceeded
estimated earnings by 3.1%. This earnings surprise percentage is below the 1-year average (+13.8%), the 5-year average
(+7.5%), the 10-year average (+8.0%), and the 15-year average (+6.7%) for a quarter. In fact, the third quarter marks the
lowest earnings surprise percentage reported by the index since Q4 2022 (+1.3%). What is driving the lower earnings
surprise percentage for the S&P 500 for Q3?

At the sector level, the Communication Services sector is the only sector reporting a negative earnings surprise for Q3 at
-22.1%. Within this sector, Charter Communications (-$20.50 vs. $8.94), Meta Platforms ($3.83 vs. $6.54), and Alphabet
($2.12 vs. $3.02) have reported the largest negative EPS surprises. The (GAAP) EPS actual for Meta Platforms for Q3
2026 included a $9.89 billion charge related to a litigation agreement, while the (GAAP) EPS actual for Alphabet included
a loss of $8.7 billion in other income due to net losses on equity securities. The negative EPS surprises reported by these
three companies are also the largest contributors to the lower earnings surprise percentage for the quarter. If these three
companies were excluded, the earnings surprise percentage for the S&P 500 for Q3 would improve to 6.7% from 3.1%.

Due to the lower earnings surprise percentage, the Q3 earnings growth rate for the S&P 500 has seen a smaller
improvement than average during this earnings season. Overall, the blended earnings growth rate has improved by 2.8
percentage points (to 31.9% from 29.1%) since September 30. This increase is below the 1-year average (+14.1
percentage points), 5-year average (+6.4 percentages points), 10-year average (+6.9 percentage points), and 15-year
average (+5.5 percentage points) improvement in the earnings growth rate during an earnings season. For more details,
please see this article: S&P 500 Likely to Report Earnings Growth Above 35% for Q3.

Despite the lower earnings surprise percentage, the market is rewarding positive EPS surprises more than average and
punishing negative EPS surprises less than average for Q3. For more details, please pages 3 to 6.

Q3 Earnings Season: By The Numbers
Overview
At this late stage of the earnings season, the S&P 500 is reporting mixed results for Q3 relative to expectations. While the
percentage of S&P 500 companies reporting positive earnings surprises is above recent averages, the magnitude of
earnings surprises is below recent averages. As a result, the index is reporting higher earnings for the third quarter today
relative to the end of last week and relative to the end of the quarter. In addition, the index is reporting (year-over-year)
earnings growth above 25% for the 3rd straight quarter.

Overall, 91% of the companies in the S&P 500 have reported actual results for Q3 2026 to date. Of these companies, 84%
have reported actual EPS above estimates, which is above the 5-year average of 78% and above the 10-year average of
77%. In aggregate, companies are reporting earnings that are 3.1% above estimates, which is below the 5-year average
of 7.5% and below the 10-year average of 8.0%. If 3.1% is the actual number for the quarter, it will mark the lowest earnings
surprise percentage reported by the index since Q4 2022 (1.3%). Historical averages reflect actual results from all 500
companies, not the actual results from the percentage of companies that have reported through this point in time.

During the past week, positive EPS surprises reported by companies in multiple sectors (led by a company in the
Communication Services sector) were the largest contributors to the increase in the overall earnings growth rate for the
index over this period. Since September 30, positive EPS surprises reported by companies in multiple sectors (led by
companies in the Energy, Information Technology, and Financials sectors), partially offset by negative EPS surprises
reported by companies in the Communication Services sector, have been the largest contributors to the increase in the
overall earnings growth rate for the index over this period.

As a result, the index is reporting higher earnings for the third quarter today relative to the end of last week and relative to
the end of the quarter. The blended (combines actual results for companies that have reported and estimated results for
companies that have yet to report) earnings growth rate for the third quarter is 31.9% today, compared to an earnings
growth rate of 31.3% last week and an earnings growth rate of 29.1% at the end of the third quarter (September 30).

If 31.9% is the actual growth rate for the quarter, it will mark the third consecutive quarter of earnings growth above 25%
and the eighth consecutive quarter of double-digit earnings growth for the index.

All eleven sectors are reporting (or have reported) year-over-year earnings growth. Seven of these sectors are reporting
(or have reported) double-digit growth, led by the Energy, Information Technology, and Materials sectors.

In terms of revenues, 74% of S&P 500 companies have reported actual revenues above estimates, which is above the 5-
year average of 70% and above the 10-year average of 68%. In aggregate, companies are reporting revenues that are
2.2% above the estimates, which is above the 5-year average of 1.8% and above the 10-year average of 1.6%. Again,
historical averages reflect actual results from all 500 companies, not the actual results from the percentage of companies
that have reported through this point in time.

During the past week, positive revenue surprises reported by companies in the Financials and Utilities sectors were the
largest contributors to the increase in the overall revenue growth rate for the index over this period. Since September 30,
positive revenue surprises reported by companies in the Energy, Health Care, and Information Technology sectors have
been the largest contributors to the increase in the overall revenue growth rate for the index over this period.

As a result, the blended revenue growth rate for the third quarter is 14.4% today, compared to a revenue growth rate of
14.3% last week and a revenue growth rate of 12.2% at the end of the third quarter (September 30).
