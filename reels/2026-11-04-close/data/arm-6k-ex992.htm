Dear Shareholder,

Arm delivered a record second quarter, reflecting strong execution and growing demand for the Arm compute platform. Revenue reached $1.44 billion, up 27% year over year, marking our fifth consecutive quarter of year over year growth exceeding 20%. License and other revenue grew 50% to $773 million, while royalty revenue grew 8% to $670 million, with data center royalties doubling year over year. Non-GAAP earnings per share reached $0.52, up 33% year over year, while we continued to increase investment in R&D.

AI is driving unprecedented demand for compute across the cloud, devices and autonomous systems, with Arm being the compute platform of choice. Our business is now driven by two areas, both of which are showing rapid growth: The IP business, where partners increase their investment in Arm technology and deploy record numbers of Arm-based silicon, and our new silicon business, where demand for Arm AGI CPU is extremely strong. Both are built on the same Arm compute platform, leveraging a common software ecosystem.

As customers integrate increasingly powerful CPUs, GPUs and NPUs, into their designs, they face both growing system complexity and increasingly demanding power constraints. This is driving greater investment in Arm technology by our customers, contributing to 50% growth in license and other revenue. Strong licensing momentum is an important leading indicator of future royalty growth as these new designs enter production where we expect royalty revenue to accelerate.

Arm Is Gaining Share Across the Data Center

Arm continues to gain share across the data center, driven by growing demand for performance and power efficiency as AI infrastructure scales. Data center royalties doubled year over year, and Arm-based server market share at the top hyperscalers more than tripled—from 15% in calendar 2024 to more than 50% in calendar 2025. Based on current ramps across Google Axion, AWS Graviton, Microsoft Cobalt, and NVIDIA platforms, we expect that share to exceed 60% by the end of calendar 2026.

As AI moves from prompt-driven applications to persistent agents, power efficient CPUs are becoming increasingly critical to data center performance. Agents execute software, call tools and coordinate work between accelerator invocations, increasing demand for both Arm-based processors developed by our partners and Arm AGI CPU.

Our partners continue to expand their deployment of Arm-based infrastructure. Amazon recently reported that Graviton revenue commitments increased more than 20x year over year, Microsoft highlighted strong adoption of Arm-based Cobalt CPUs, and Google identified Arm-based Axion as a critical component of its AI infrastructure. NVIDIA continues to expand its Arm-based Vera CPU platform for agentic AI, now generally available on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) and deployed in production with OpenAI.

During the quarter, we introduced Neoverse CSS N4 enabling partners to develop differentiated silicon for CPUs, networking, infrastructure management, and security. Delivering up to 2x performance per socket and 25% better performance per watt than the prior generation, CSS N4 enables more compute within constrained data centers.

Alongside our growing IP business, Arm AGI CPU expands our opportunity by delivering Arm-designed silicon directly to customers. Since launching in March, demand has continued to strengthen, with purchase orders secured from multiple customers extending through calendar 2027 and first commercial deliveries on track for fiscal Q4’27. Oracle Cloud Infrastructure plans to offer Arm AGI CPU instances in 2027, bringing Arm-designed and partner-designed processors together on a common platform and software ecosystem.

Our confidence in achieving up to $2 billion in Arm AGI CPU revenue over FY27–28 has increased over the last 90 days, supported by additional supply and customer mix. We remain focused on expanding capacity to meet growing customer demand.

Power availability is becoming a critical constraint on data center growth, with Morgan Stanley recently projecting a 38GW global power shortfall through 2028. Arm is helping customers address this constraint by delivering more useful compute from the power already available. At the Open Compute Project summit, independent testing by Signal65 showed Arm AGI CPU delivering approximately 2x the performance per rack versus AMD EPYC 9755, which could reduce the projected global power shortfall by half.

As agentic AI scales, infrastructure must also become more secure. NVIDIA's recently announced OpenShell architecture uses Arm-based CPUs to execute AI workloads and Arm-based BlueField-4 Data Processing Units (DPUs) to independently enforce security and isolation. With Arm compute on both sides of the trust boundary, this demonstrates the expanding role of the Arm platform in enabling agentic AI at scale.

Arm Is Enabling the Next Generation of Edge AI

AI is transforming personal computing, bringing more intelligence directly to PCs and smartphones while increasing demand for compute performance, power efficiency and security. As AI agents become more capable and persistent, Arm provides a common platform and software foundation spanning devices and the cloud.

Arm-based PCs have now reached approximately 20% of global shipments, reflecting growing adoption of Arm in personal computing. Google introduced Googlebook, a new category of AI-native laptops built for Gemini Intelligence, with Arm serving as a primary compute platform for many models. NVIDIA’s RTX Spark, built on Arm further expands our reach into local AI, content creation and gaming.

In smartphones, AI is driving demand for more capable on-device processing without compromising battery life. Arm CSS for Mobile 2 drives the performance of agentic AI workloads up by 70% through SME2, already adopted by major smartphone vendors like OPPO and vivo. Arm’s first AI-native Mali GPU, G2-Ultra NX, delivers up to 4x the frame rate at the same sustained power, with Xiaomi adopting the technology and leading gaming developers integrating Arm Neural Technology.

As AI agents access more personal information and act on users’ behalf, Armv9 security technologies help protect sensitive workloads and system resources. By enabling more AI compute within the power constraints of personal devices, Arm is expanding both its market opportunity and the value of its technology in every chip.

Physical AI Is Scaling on Arm

Physical AI royalty revenue grew 50% year over year, driven by increased adoption of advanced driver-assistance and in-vehicle infotainment systems. As vehicles become more intelligent and autonomous, demand for compute performance, real-time control, safety and security continues to increase. Armv9 and Zena CSS provide the foundation for this transition, with Tensor’s Level 4 personal Robocar integrating more than 400 Arm CPU cores to support autonomy, safety and vehicle control.

As automotive architectures evolve toward more centralized computing and higher levels of autonomy, Arm is positioned to capture increasing compute content per vehicle. Beyond automotive, increasingly capable robots and autonomous machines are creating new demand for energy-efficient compute and real-time processing.

To accelerate adoption, we launched Arm Total Design for Physical AI, bringing together more than 80 companies across compute, software, AI models and system integration to accelerate the development and deployment of Physical AI systems. This initiative extends Arm’s compute platform into new markets as AI moves beyond digital applications into the physical world.

More Compute, More Value for Arm

For the first time, our partners reported more than 10 billion Arm-based chips shipped during the quarter, an increase of 1.5 billion chips year over year. This adds to the scale of a platform already used by more than 30 million developers.

Q2 FYE27 Financial Overview
•Arm's Q2 results demonstrated strong year-over-year revenue growth. Total revenue increased 27% year-over-year to $1,443 million, representing our best ever second-quarter revenue.
•Royalty revenue increased 8% year-over-year to $670 million, driven by the continued adoption of Arm technology with higher royalty rates per chip, such as Armv9 architecture and Arm CSS, and increased deployment of Arm-based chips in data centers. This was partially offset by the impact of memory pricing on the smartphone market.
•License and other revenue increased 50% year-over-year to $773 million driven by continued strong demand for Arm IP, as well as fluctuation in the timing and size of multiple high-value license agreements and contributions from backlog.
•Annualized contract value (ACV), a metric for normalized license and other revenue, increased 12% year-over-year to $1,790 million.
•GAAP gross profit was $1,401 million, which equates to a GAAP gross margin of 97.1%. Non-GAAP gross profit was $1,412 million, which equates to a non-GAAP gross margin of 97.9%.
•GAAP operating expenses were $1,200 million. Non-GAAP operating expenses were $774 million and increased 19% year-over-year.
•GAAP research and development was $857 million. Non-GAAP research and development was $547 million and increased 17% year-over-year driven primarily by investment in engineering headcount and other expenses.
•GAAP selling, general and administrative was $343 million. Non-GAAP selling, general and administrative was $227 million and increased 25% year-over-year.
•GAAP operating income was $201 million. Non-GAAP operating income was $638 million.
•GAAP operating margin decreased to 13.9% from 14.4% in the prior year period. Non-GAAP operating margin increased to 44.2% from 41.1% in the same period a year ago.
•GAAP net income was $130 million and GAAP fully diluted earnings per share ("EPS") was $0.12 compared with $0.22 in the same period a year ago. Non-GAAP net income was $566 million and non-GAAP fully diluted EPS was $0.52 compared with $0.39 in the same period a year ago.
•Non-GAAP free cash flow (FCF) for the trailing twelve months was $1,096 million.
•Cash and cash equivalents and short-term investments totaled $3,621 million.

Total revenue
Our major product offerings consist of the following:
License and other revenue
•Intellectual property license — We generally license IP under non-exclusive license agreements that provide usage rights for specific applications for a finite or perpetual term. These licenses are made available electronically to address the customer-specific business requirements. These arrangements generally have distinct performance obligations that consist of transferring the licensed IPs, version extensions of architecture IP or releases of specified IPs, and support services. Support services consist of a stand-ready obligation to provide technical support, patches, and bug fixes over the support term. Where arrangements include royalty buy-downs or upfront minimum royalty commitments, the Company treats them as fixed contract consideration under licensing revenue. Revenue allocated to the IP license is recognized at a point in time upon the delivery or beginning of the license term, whichever is later. Revenue allocated to distinct version extensions of architecture IP or releases of specified IP, excluding when-and-if-available minor updates over the support term, are recognized at a point in time upon the delivery or beginning of license term, whichever is later.
Certain license agreements provide customers with the right to access a library of current and future IPs on an unlimited basis over the contractual period depending on the terms of the applicable contract. These licensing arrangements represent stand-ready obligations in that the timing of the delivery of the underlying IPs is within the control of the customer and the extent of use in any given period does not diminish the remaining performance obligation. The contract consideration related to these arrangements is recognized ratably over the term of the contract in line with when the control of the performance obligations is transferred.
Certain subscription license agreements include unspecified future IPs that are provided on a when-and-if-available basis, representing a stand-ready obligation. The contract consideration allocated to the stand-ready obligation is recognized on a ratable basis over the term of the contract, commencing upon the later of the effective date of the agreement and the transfer of the initial available IP license.
•Software sales, including development systems — Sales of software, including development systems, which are not specifically designed for a given license (such as off-the-shelf software), are recognized upon delivery when control has been transferred and the customer can begin to use and benefit from the license.
•Professional services — Services (such as training, professional and design services) that we provide, which are not essential to the functionality of the IP, are separately stated and priced in the contract and accounted for separately. Training revenue is recognized as services are performed. In certain arrangements, we also provide customers with professional and design services, as a stand-ready obligation, that are recognized on a ratable basis over the term of the contract.
•Support and maintenance — Support and maintenance is a stand-ready obligation to the customer that is both provided and consumed simultaneously. Revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis over the period for which support and maintenance is contractually agreed pursuant to the license.
•Third-party arrangements — In certain arrangements, the Company arranges for third parties to provide products or services to customers. Revenue is recognized on a gross basis when the Company controls the product or service, and on a net margin basis when it acts as an agent. In agent arrangements, control passes directly from the third party to the customer, and the Company bears no inventory or performance risk. For product-related agent arrangements, revenue is recognized at a point in time when the customer obtains control of the underlying products.
Royalty revenue
For most IP license agreements, royalties are collected on products that incorporate our IP. Royalties are recognized on an accrual basis in the quarter in which the customer ships their products, based on our technology that it contains. This estimation process for the royalty revenue accrual is based on a combination of methodologies, including the use of historical sales trends and macroeconomic factors for predictive analysis, the analysis of customer royalty reports and their sales trends and forecasts, as well as data and forecasts from third-party industry research providers. Data considered includes revenue, unit shipments, average selling price, product mix, market share and market penetration. Adjustments to revenue are required in subsequent periods to reflect changes in estimates as new information becomes available, primarily

resulting from actual amounts subsequently reported by the licensees in the period following the accrual, including royalty audit resolutions.
Annualized contract value ("ACV")
Each quarter, we track the ACV relating to licensing agreements signed with our customers and those contracted through Arm China per the aggregate license fee as shared under the Intellectual Property License Agreement with Arm China. We define ACV as the total annualized committed fees, excluding any potential future royalty revenue, for all signed agreements deemed to be active through the last day of each applicable reporting period. Arm Total Access agreements and Architecture License Agreements are deemed to be active for, and annualized over, the number of years in the contract. Any other license agreements, including single use and limited use licenses issued under an Arm Flexible Access agreement or Technology License Agreement, are deemed to be active for, and annualized over, three years based on the historical licensing patterns of our customers. The aggregate license fee shared by Arm China is also deemed to be active for, and annualized over, three years.
ACV is an operational metric based on committed fees, excluding royalties, not recognized revenue, and therefore is not reconcilable to, nor a substitute for, revenue reported under GAAP. However, we consider ACV to be a key operational metric that we use to track existing licensing commitments with our customers. Bookings of new licenses and recognized revenue may fluctuate materially from quarter to quarter due to customer buying patterns, timing of subscription renewals and as a function of contract duration. As a result, we believe ACV provides an additional understanding of our business performance and long-term trends.
Non-GAAP financial measures
In addition to our results determined in accordance with GAAP, we utilize and present financial measures that are not calculated and presented in accordance with GAAP. Our non-GAAP financial measures include non-GAAP cost of sales, non-GAAP gross profit (loss), non-GAAP gross margin, non-GAAP research and development operating expenses, non-GAAP selling, general and administrative operating expenses, non-GAAP disposal, restructuring and other operating expenses, net, non-GAAP operating expenses, non-GAAP operating income (loss), non-GAAP operating margin, non-GAAP income (loss) from equity investments, net, non-GAAP interest income, net, non-GAAP other non-operating income (loss), net, non-GAAP income (loss) before income taxes, non-GAAP income tax benefit (expense), non-GAAP net income (loss), non-GAAP basic and diluted net income per share, or earnings per share, attributable to ordinary shareholders, non-GAAP free cash flow, and non-GAAP free cash flow TTM. We believe these non-GAAP financial measures provide useful information to investors and others in understanding and evaluating our results of operations, as well as provide a useful measure for period-to-period comparisons of our business performance. Moreover, we have included these non-GAAP financial measures because they are key measurements used by our management internally to make operating decisions, including those related to analyzing operating expenses, evaluating performance, and performing strategic planning and annual budgeting. We believe that the presentation of our non-GAAP financial measures, when viewed holistically, is helpful to investors in assessing the consistency and comparability of our performance in relation to prior periods and facilitates comparisons of our financial performance relative to our competitors, particularly with respect to competitors that present similar non-GAAP financial measures in addition to their GAAP results.
Non-GAAP financial measures are presented for supplemental information purposes only, should not be considered a substitute for financial information presented in accordance with GAAP, and may not align with similar financial measures presented by our competitors, which may limit the ability of investors to assess our performance relative to certain peer companies.
Non-GAAP financial measures (other than non-GAAP free cash flow and non-GAAP free cash flow TTM) presented herein exclude acquisition related expenses, share-based compensation ("SBC") cost associated with equity-classified awards where our intent is to issue equity upon vesting (in lieu of cash settlement), employer taxes related to SBC equity-classified awards, net of the research and development ("R&D") tax incentives associated with these taxes, other operating income (expenses), net, costs associated with disposal activities, gain on disposal of business, (income) loss from equity investments, net, and income tax effect on non-GAAP adjustments. Non-GAAP free cash flow and non-GAAP free cash flow TTM exclude purchases of property and equipment, purchases of intangible assets, and payment of intangible asset obligations. We exclude these items from our non-GAAP financial measures because they are non-cash or non-recurring in nature, or because the amount and timing of these items is unpredictable and not driven by core results of operations, which renders comparisons with prior periods and competitors less meaningful.
