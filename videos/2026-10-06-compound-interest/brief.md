# Compound interest

Money 101, episode 1. Series: `money-101`.

## The idea

"Interés compuesto: por qué el tiempo pesa más que la cantidad." The first episode of Money 101,
the playlist of basics, for a general audience (not only the United States).

## Verdict

**make it.** It is the idea every later episode leans on (inflation, debt, fees and funds are
all compounding), it is a mechanism that can be drawn, and it can be told with arithmetic alone:
the only facts that need a source are what it is, the rule of 72 and how few people get it
right. Nothing in it needs a forecast or a recommendation.

## The angle

Compound interest is growth on top of earlier growth, so it does almost nothing for years and
then most of the result arrives at the end: which is why the time money is left alone does more
than the amount put in.

## Who it is for, and what they will know at the end

People in their twenties, anywhere, who have heard "start early" and never seen why. At the end
they can say what compound interest is, why its curve bends, how to estimate a doubling time in
their head, and that the same arithmetic runs against them in a debt.

## The facts

From sources:

- Compound interest is "the interest you earn on interest". 100 at 5 percent a year is 105 after
  one year and 110.25 after two: 5 on the deposit and 0.25 on the first year's interest. Left
  alone it is more than 162 after 10 years and almost 340 after 25. | https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/information/youth/teachers-classroom-resources/what-compound-interest
- The rule of 72: divide 72 by the yearly rate to get roughly how many years a sum takes to
  double (at 9 percent, about every 8 years). | https://www.investor.gov/additional-resources/information/youth/teachers-classroom-resources/what-compound-interest
- "Only 42% of adults can correctly answer a question about compound interest, on average across
  participating countries and economies", and 46 percent among adults who hold a savings
  product. The survey covers 39 countries and economies, 20 of them OECD members; published 14
  December 2023 (the sentences are on pages 7 and 12 of the report). | https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2023/12/oecd-infe-2023-international-survey-of-adult-financial-literacy_8ce94e2c.html
- The line that compound interest is "the eighth wonder of the world" is very unlikely to be
  Einstein's: no substantive evidence that he said it; the earliest close matches are
  advertisements (one in the Riverside Daily Press, 1916). Optional, as an aside. | https://quoteinvestigator.com/2011/10/31/compound-interest/
- Returns are not a fixed rate: "Global stocks provided an annualized real return of 3.5%" in
  the 21st century, lower than in the 20th (UBS Global Investment Returns Yearbook 2025,
  release of 4 March 2025). For the caveat only, never as what to expect. | https://www.ubs.com/global/en/media/display-page-ndp/en-20250304-global-investment-returns-yearbook-2025.html

Arithmetic, worked out here (no source needed; the rate is an example, said as one every time):

- 1,000 left alone at 5 percent a year: 1,629 after 10 years, 2,653 after 20, 4,322 after 30,
  7,040 after 40. With simple interest (50 a year, never added to the sum) it is 3,000.
- The last ten years add 2,718: more than the first twenty together (1,653). The sum reaches
  half of its final value only in year 26.
- Doubling: about every 14 years at 5 percent (72 / 5 = 14.4; exactly 14.2). At 2 percent, 36
  years; at 7 percent, about 10.
- 100 a month for 40 years at 5 percent: 148,252, of which 48,000 was paid in. Starting ten
  years later (30 years): 81,538, with 36,000 paid in. The first ten years of deposits, 12,000,
  a quarter of the money, become 66,715: 45 percent of the total. (At 3 percent they are 37
  percent of it; at 7 percent, 53.) To end in the same place after starting ten years late, the
  monthly amount has to be 1.8 times as large.
- A debt at 20 percent a year that is never paid down doubles in under four years (3.8).

Not to be used: a "5.2 percent real since 1900" figure for world equities appears in press
coverage of the UBS yearbook, but I could not find it on a UBS page I could read.

## The strongest objection

That the tidy curve is a fiction: no one earns a fixed 5 percent for 40 years, inflation eats
part of the 7,040, fees and taxes compound against it, and markets can lose for a decade. All
true, and the video says so: the rate is an example and not a promise, the shape (slow, then
fast) is what holds at any positive rate, and inflation and fees get their own episodes. The
second objection is to the series' own rule: "start early" is advice. The video does not say
it; it shows what ten years do to the same sum and leaves it there.

## Outline

About 5 minutes, some 25 scenes of 4 to 15 seconds. Numbers are shown with no currency sign: the
arithmetic is the same in any currency, and the narration says so once.

1. **01-hook** (presenter). A thousand, left alone at five percent for forty years: how much is
   there? Shows the question; then 7,040 counts up.
2. **02-survey**. Fewer than half of adults get a question like that right: 42 percent, in 39
   countries (OECD). Shows the figure, with its source.
3. **03-title**. Title card: Compound interest. Money 101, episode 1.
4. **04-year-one**. Year one: 1,000 earns 50. Shows a bar of 1,000 and a slice of 50 on top.
5. **05-year-two**. Year two: the 50 earns too. 52.50, and that extra 2.50 is the whole idea:
   interest on interest. Shows the slice of the slice, marked.
6. **06-simple-or-compound** (presenter). Two ways to pay interest: on what you put in, or on
   everything that is there.
7. **07-two-lines**. Forty years, two lines drawn: simple ends at 3,000, compound at 7,040.
   Same rate, same thousand.
8. **08-decades**. Four bars, one per decade: 1,629, 2,653, 4,322, 7,040.
9. **09-last-decade**. The last ten years add more than the first twenty. Shows the two gains
   side by side: 2,718 against 1,653.
10. **10-half-way**. Half of the final sum arrives after year 26. Shows the curve with the
    half-way mark far to the right.
11. **11-slow-then-fast** (presenter). So for a long time it looks like nothing is happening.
    That is the part people give up in.
12. **12-rule-of-72**. A shortcut: 72 divided by the rate is the years to double. Shows 72 / 5.
13. **13-doublings**. At 5 percent: 1,000, 2,000, 4,000 in 14, 28, 42 years. Shows the steps.
14. **14-rates**. At 2 percent, 36 years to double; at 7, about 10. Small differences in rate
    are large differences in time.
15. **15-time-or-amount** (presenter). Which matters more, how much goes in or for how long?
16. **16-forty-years**. 100 a month for 40 years at 5 percent: 48,000 paid in, about 148,000 at
    the end. Shows what was paid and what it earned, stacked.
17. **17-ten-years-late**. The same, starting ten years later: 36,000 paid in, about 82,000.
18. **18-first-ten**. The first ten years of deposits, a quarter of the money, are almost half
    of the result. Shows that slice of the bar, marked.
19. **19-catching-up**. To end in the same place ten years late, the monthly amount has to be
    1.8 times as large.
20. **20-both-ways** (presenter). None of this is on your side by nature. It works for whoever
    is owed the interest.
21. **21-debt**. A debt at 20 percent that is not paid down doubles in under four years. Shows
    the same curve, in the colour of a figure that goes against you.
22. **22-not-a-promise**. Five percent was an example. Real returns move: global stocks, after
    inflation, 3.5 percent a year this century (UBS). And prices rise meanwhile.
23. **23-einstein** (optional). You will hear Einstein called it the eighth wonder of the
    world. There is no evidence he did; the oldest versions are adverts.
24. **24-recap**. Three things: interest on interest; slow, then fast; 72 over the rate.
25. **25-close** (presenter). Next: inflation, the same arithmetic working against a banknote.

## The Short

Scenes 01-hook, 08-decades and 09-last-decade: the question, the four decades, and the line
that the last ten years add more than the first twenty. About 40 seconds, one whole idea, and it
needs nothing from the scenes left out.

## Decided after the brief (2026-10-06)

The user: figures in **dollars**, and the example rate is **8 percent**, not 5. Left to the
editor: the Einstein aside is out (ten seconds the video does not need) and the Short is the one
proposed. So the arithmetic of the video, at 8 percent a year, is:

- 1,000 dollars left alone: 2,159 after 10 years, 4,661 after 20, 10,063 after 30, 21,725 after
  40. With simple interest, 4,200. Year one earns 80; year two 86.40, of which 6.40 is interest
  on interest.
- The last ten years add 11,662: more than the first thirty together (9,063). Half of the final
  sum is reached in year 31.
- Doubling: every 9 years (72 / 8). At 12 percent, 6; at 4 percent, 18; at 2 percent, 36.
- 100 dollars a month for 40 years: 322,108, of which 48,000 was paid in. Starting ten years
  later: 140,855, with 36,000 paid in. The first ten years of deposits, 12,000, become 181,253:
  56 percent of the total. To end in the same place ten years late takes about 230 a month.
- A debt of 1,000 at 20 percent with nothing paid off: 2,074 after 4 years, 4,300 after 8.

Eight percent is well above what the one sourced figure in the video shows (3.5 percent a year
after inflation for global stocks this century, UBS): the video says so, in scene
22-not-a-promise, and every slide with arithmetic carries "an example, not a forecast".
