## 01-hook

This is My Hub. The private side of Market Hub, where your own stocks live. In the next seven minutes you will see everything it does today, and what I would love you to try. Stay until the end. I have a favour to ask.

## 02-map

My Hub has four pages of your own. Overview. Analysis. Watchlist. And Community. Around them, four tools to dig into a company. And a page where you see, and can delete, everything that is kept about you.

## 03-sign-in

Start at the market hub dot app, and press Sign in. You can come in with your Google account. Or create an account with an email and a password of at least ten characters. One warning. There is no forgot my password yet. So pick one you will remember.

## 04-portfolio

First stop, Portfolio. Search a company by name or by ticker, and add it. For each position, type how many shares you hold and, if you like, the average price you paid. That price is optional. It is only there to show your gain or loss.

## 05-watchlist-add

Below it, the watchlist. Stocks you follow without owning them. Add a handful. Then save, and the dashboard opens. To test, you do not need your real portfolio. Any five or six stocks will do.

## 06-overview

This is Overview. Four figures on top. What the portfolio is worth. What it did today. Your gain against what you paid. And how these holdings did over the last year. Under them, your portfolio is read back to you in plain sentences. Concentration. Sectors. Countries. Dividends.

## 07-overview-chart

Then the chart. Your holdings against the index, from one month to one year. Switch the period. Add the Nasdaq, the Dow or the Russell. One thing to know. Market Hub does not record your trades. So this is what you hold today, held through the whole period. It is not your real past return.

## 08-positions

Next, every position on one line. Shares, cost, price, today's move, weight and gain. Further down, what the portfolio is made of, and your watchlist.

## 09-analysis

Analysis takes that same portfolio apart. The same positions, gathered four ways. By sector. By country. By volatility. And by size. Each group with its weight, and how it has done.

## 10-analysis-risk

Then, period by period, your holdings next to four indices. And how the portfolio moves. Its volatility. Its beta. Its deepest fall from a peak. And how its weight is spread, down to how many equal positions it really behaves like.

## 11-analysis-today

Today, position by position. Which ones pushed the portfolio up, which ones pulled it down, and by how much. And at the bottom, every position in one table. Press any column to sort by it.

## 12-watch-charts

Watchlist is for looking at many stocks at once. It opens on a wall of charts. You choose how many at a time, from one to twelve. The span, from three months to two years. Candles or a line. And three moving averages.

## 13-watch-chips

On top, every stock is a chip. Switch it on or off. Bring in the stocks you hold. Or look at any other stock, without adding it to your list.

## 14-readings

Now press Readings. Each stock gets one sentence that sums it up. Under it, a gauge for each angle. Price against its fifty day average. Trend. And strength against the index.

## 15-readings-more

Keep going. Its sector. Momentum. The fifty two week range. Volume. Growth, as the analysts' consensus has it. And price to earnings. It describes where each figure stands. It never tells you what to do.

## 16-watch-map

And Map puts the whole list on one plane. Left to right, how far each price is from its fifty day average. Bottom to top, how it has done against the index over three months. Press a dot, and its full reading opens at the side.

## 17-share

Community is where it stops being private. But only if you say so. Sharing is off until you turn it on. Pick a name. Others see that name, the stocks you hold, and their weights in percent. Nobody sees your real name, your email, how many shares you own, or what they are worth.

## 18-board

Once you share, you are on the board. It ranks the shared portfolios by period, next to the indices and the average. And it tells you where you stand. The names you see here are demo accounts. The real board is almost empty. That is where you come in.

## 19-competition

The other half of Community is a monthly competition. A game, with no money in it. You build a portfolio before the month starts. It stays fixed for the whole month. The best return wins. The first month is November, and entries are open until October thirty first.

## 20-entry

Building an entry takes a minute. Three to ten single stocks. No funds. Each one between five and fifty percent, adding up to one hundred. Pick a name to play under, and send it. You can change it until the month begins. And every month has its own discussion.

## 21-fundamentals

Now the tools, in the menu on the left. Fundamentals. Type any American company, and get its key numbers. Its statements, year by year. Its valuation. Its technicals. And a comparison, side by side, with other companies.

## 22-earnings

Earnings reads the latest results release of a company. Was guidance raised or lowered. What changed since the last one. And what stands out. Market trends adds it all up, quarter by quarter.

## 23-peers

Peers is a map of about fifteen hundred American companies. Two dots are close when the companies describe their business in similar words. Find one, and see which companies sound most like it.

## 24-playground

And Playground is an empty board. Ask for a chart, a comparison or a table, in your own words, and it builds it. Nothing on it is saved.

## 25-account

One last page. Account and data. It lists everything Market Hub keeps about you. You can download it. Change your password. Or delete your account, and all of it goes at once.

## 26-test

So here is what I would like you to do. One. Sign in, and add a few stocks. Two. Read Overview and Analysis, and check that the numbers make sense. Three. Play with the Watchlist. Four. Share a portfolio, and enter the November competition. Five. Open at least one tool.

## 27-feedback

Then tell me what you found. Anything that broke. Anything that was slow. Any number that looked wrong. And anything you did not understand, because that is a bug too. On a phone or on a computer. Both help.

## 28-app

And now the favour. I am building My Hub as a phone app, for Android and for iPhone. The same account. The same portfolio. In your pocket.

## 29-testers

Before Google lets a new app into its store, it asks for twelve people to test it, for fourteen days in a row. So I need twelve testers. And I would be very grateful if you were one of them.

## 30-how

If you are up for it, send me a message with three things. Your name. Whether your phone is an Android or an iPhone. And the email of the Google account on that phone, the one you use in the Play Store. On an iPhone, the email of your Apple account. That email is how the store invites you. I will add you, and send you the link when the app is ready.

## 31-close

That is My Hub. Thank you for watching, and thank you for testing. Now go and break something.
