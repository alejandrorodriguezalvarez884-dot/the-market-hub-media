## 01-coffee

Everyone has heard it. Your money goes on the five-dollar coffee. So here's a question. Of every hundred dollars an average household in the United States spends, how much goes on eating out? All of it. Restaurants. Takeaway. And the coffee.

## 02-guess

Take a guess. Pause it if you like. Five dollars? Twelve? Or twenty-five?

## 03-answer

About five. Five dollars out of every hundred. That's every meal and every coffee bought away from home.

## 04-housing

Now housing. Rent or a mortgage, the bills, the things in it. That's about thirty-three dollars of every hundred. A third of everything.

## 05-shape

So the coffee is a sliver. The big slices decide the shape of your month. And that's what a budget is really about.

## 06-title
seconds: 3

## 07-diet

The word budget sounds like a diet. Cutting. Guilt. A long list of things you can't have. But that's not what it is. A diet is about less. A budget is about where. That's the difference.

## 08-pizza

Picture a pizza, shared between friends. Before anyone grabs, somebody cuts it. Everyone knows their slice. A budget does that with your money. It cuts the pizza before the month eats it.

## 09-plan

The consumer protection bureau in the United States puts it plainly. A budget is a plan. The money you expect to receive, and how it will be spent or saved. It even has a second name. A spending plan.

## 10-net

And the pizza is your net pay. Not the number on the offer. What actually lands in your account, after tax and contributions. That's last episode.

## 11-look-back

So where does it start? With the past. The consumer bureau suggests going through your account and card history for the last several months. Not to judge it. To see how the pizza was actually eaten, before deciding how to cut it.

## 12-fixed

The first slices are fixed costs. They come every month, and cost about the same. Rent. A phone plan. A loan payment.

## 13-variable

Then the variable costs. They change from month to month. Groceries. Getting around. Going out. These are the slices that move the most.

## 14-yearly

Then the ones that hide. Bills that come once a year. Insurance. A repair. Presents. Six hundred dollars a year is fifty a month. So it gets a slice like the rest. Spread over twelve months, it stops being a surprise.

## 15-leftover

And what's left is a slice too. Saving. Paying off a debt faster. In a budget it has a name and a size. It isn't just whatever survives the month.

## 16-us

So how does the average household cut its pizza? In the United States, housing takes about a third. Getting around, about seventeen percent. And food, about thirteen. These are averages, and nobody is the average. But they show which slices are large.

## 17-eu

In the European Union, housing and its bills take almost a quarter. Food, about thirteen percent. And transport, about thirteen.

## 18-half

Three slices. Somewhere to live. A way to get around. Something to eat. Together, they take about half of everything in the EU. And more than that in the United States.

## 19-coffee-math

Back to the coffee. Five dollars every workday comes to a little over a hundred dollars a month. The average American household spends about twenty-two hundred a month on housing.

## 20-big-decisions

That's why the big slices are set by a few big decisions. Where you live. How you get around. You make those once in a while, and they weigh for years. The small ones, you make every day, and each one weighs very little.

## 21-recut

Then the month happens. A slice runs over. The car needs a repair. A budget doesn't break when that happens. The pizza gets cut again. Some of one slice moves to another.

## 22-varies

And if your pay changes from month to month? In the United States, fifty-eight percent of the self-employed said their income varies. And twenty-eight percent of employees. One way budgets handle it is to start from a lean month.

## 23-size

But a budget only shares out the pizza. It can't make it bigger. Forty-one percent of American adults said they always or often have money left at the end of the month.

## 24-income

But that depends a lot on income. Under twenty-five thousand dollars a year, it was nineteen percent. Above a hundred thousand, fifty-nine. Same idea of a budget. Very different pizzas.

## 25-recap

Three things to keep. One: a budget cuts the pizza before the month eats it, and the pizza is your net pay. Two: three slices, housing, getting around and food, take about half of everything. Three: what's left is a slice too, and when the month gets messy, the pizza gets cut again.
