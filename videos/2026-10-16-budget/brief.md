# Budget

Money 101, episode 5. Series: `money-101`.

## The idea

"Prepara el siguiente vídeo de la playlist y súbelo a youtube e instagram." The fifth subject of
the list of basics the user approved on 2026-10-06 (and kept when they cut the list to ten on
2026-10-15) is a budget as a way of sharing out what arrives, not as a diet. Episode 4 announces
it as it closes: "Next time: a budget. Not as a diet. As a way of sharing out what arrives."

## Verdict

**make it.** The word "budget" sounds like cutting and guilt to most people, and the advice they
hear is about small things (the daily coffee). The mechanism can be drawn (a pizza cut before
anyone grabs a slice), the shares of a household's spending come from two statistics offices
(the BLS in the United States, Eurostat in the EU), and it needs no forecast: it says what a
budget is, what its slices are, and why the big slices weigh more than the small habits.

## The angle

A budget is deciding, before the month starts, how what arrives is shared out: a plan, not a
list of things you can't have. And in the average household three slices (housing, getting
around and food) take about half of everything, so the shape of a month is set by a few large
decisions much more than by the daily coffee.

## Who it is for, and what they will know at the end

People in their twenties, anywhere, with their first payslips, who think a budget means saying
no to things. At the end they can say what a budget is (in the CFPB's words, a plan, also called
a spending plan), that it starts from net pay, what fixed, variable and once-a-year costs are,
how the average household's spending is shared out in the US and in the EU, and why a budget
shares out what arrives but cannot make it bigger.

## The facts

From sources (all read on 2026-10-16):

- Consumer Expenditures in 2024, BLS news release of 19 December 2025: "Average annual
  expenditures for all consumer units in 2024 were $78,535". Shares of the total (Table B):
  housing 33.4 percent, transportation 17.0, food 12.9, personal insurance and pensions 12.5,
  healthcare 7.9, entertainment 4.6; "Percentages do not add to 100.0 due to rounding." Table A:
  housing 26,266 dollars; food at home 6,224; food away from home 3,945.
  | https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cesan.nr0.htm
- Household consumption by purpose, Eurostat Statistics Explained (data extracted 20 November
  2025), EU, 2024: "almost a quarter of EU household expenditure (23.6% of total ...) was devoted
  to housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels". "Other large shares are observed for food
  and non-alcoholic beverages (13.2% of the total ...) and transport (12.7% of the total ...).
  Together, these 3 categories account for almost half of the total household expenditure
  (49.5%)". Restaurants and accommodation services, 9.2 percent.
  | https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php?title=Household_consumption_by_purpose
- Financial terms glossary, Consumer Financial Protection Bureau: a budget is a plan of the money
  you expect to receive and of how it will be spent or saved over a period, "also called a
  spending plan". Fixed expenses: "Expenses, like bills, that must be paid each month and
  generally cost the same amount." Variable expenses: "Expenses that change in amount from month
  to month." Net income: what arrives "after taxes and other deductions are taken out; also
  called take-home pay." | https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/youth-financial-education/glossary/
- Assess your spending, Consumer Financial Protection Bureau: "Look at your checking account and
  credit card history for the last several months." | https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/prepare/assess-your-spending/
- Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2025, Federal Reserve Board, May 2026 (survey of
  October 2025): "In 2025, 41 percent of adults said they always or often had money left over at
  the end of the month". Nineteen percent of adults with income under 25,000 dollars, against
  59 percent with 100,000 or more (Table 18). Income that varies from month to month: 58 percent
  of the self-employed, 28 percent of those who worked for someone else.
  | https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/2025-report-economic-well-being-us-households-202605.pdf

Arithmetic, worked out here:

- Eating out in the US: 3,945 of 78,535 dollars is 5.0 percent: about five dollars of every
  hundred. Housing: 33.4 percent, about a third. Housing, transportation and food: 33.4 + 17.0 +
  12.9 = 63.3 percent in the US (the EU's three, 49.5 percent).
- An example coffee of five dollars, every workday: 5 × 5 × 52 / 12 = about 108 dollars a month.
  The average household's housing, 26,266 / 12 = about 2,190 dollars a month.
- An example bill of 600 dollars a year is 50 dollars a month.

Not used: the "50/30/20" split and other rules of thumb, which would turn the episode into a
prescription; the video says that every budget cuts the pizza its own way. Surveys of how many
people "have a budget" were not used: none was found from a primary source with a clear
question.

## The strongest objection

That this is advice in disguise: "make a budget" is telling people what to do. The video
describes what a budget is and how it works, and says plainly that people cut the pizza in
different ways; it never says how big any slice should be. The second objection is that a
budget cannot fix a pay that is too small. True, and the video says so, with the Fed's figures:
money left over at the end of the month is far more common at higher incomes. A budget shares
out what arrives; it does not make it bigger. The third is that averages hide everyone: no one
is the average household. The video says it is an average, and uses it only to show which
slices are large.

decided for the series, and the EU's shares beside the US ones. Each part has its tone of
colour.

3. **03-answer**. About five (BLS). Shows five of a hundred dots.
4. **04-housing**. Housing: about a third. Shows thirty-three dots in another colour.
5. **05-shape**. The big slices decide the shape of the month, not the coffee. Shows the two.

Part two, what a budget is (plum):

6. **06-title**. Title card: A budget, not a diet. Money 101, episode 5.
7. **07-diet**. The word sounds like a diet: cutting, guilt, a list of no. Shows it crossed.
8. **08-pizza**. A pizza shared among friends: you cut it before anyone grabs. Shows the cuts.

10. **10-net**. The pizza is net pay, what arrives (episode 4). Shows gross shrinking to net.
11. **11-look-back**. It starts with the past: the CFPB suggests going through the account and
    card history of the last several months, to see how the pizza was eaten. Shows statements.

Part three, the slices (forest):

12. **12-fixed**. Fixed costs: about the same each month (CFPB). Rent, a phone plan, a loan.
13. **13-variable**. Variable costs: change month to month. Groceries, getting around, fun.
14. **14-yearly**. The once-a-year bills: 600 a year is 50 a month, a slice like the rest.
15. **15-leftover**. What's left is a slice too, with a name and a size: saving, paying a debt.
16. **16-us**. The average US household's pie (BLS 2024): housing 33, transport 17, food 13.
17. **17-eu**. The EU's (Eurostat 2024): housing almost a quarter, food 13, transport 13.
18. **18-half**. Three slices, about half or more of everything, on both sides of the Atlantic.
19. **19-coffee-math**. The example coffee: about 108 dollars a month. Housing: about 2,190.

Part four, when the month happens (ember):

20. **20-big-decisions**. The big slices are set by a few decisions made once in a while (where
    you live, how you get around); the small ones every day.
21. **21-recut**. Then the month happens and a slice runs over: the pizza is re-cut, slice from
    slice. Shows a slice moving.
22. **22-varies**. Pay that varies: 58 percent of the self-employed, 28 of employees (Fed). One
    way budgets handle it: start from a lean month.
23. **23-size**. A budget shares out the pizza, it does not make it bigger: 41 percent always or
    often have money left over (Fed).
24. **24-income**. It depends on income: 19 percent under 25,000 dollars, 59 at 100,000 or more.

Part five, the recap (indigo):

25. **25-recap** (presenter). Three things: cut the pizza before the month eats it, from net
    pay; three slices are about half; what's left is a slice too, and the plan gets re-cut.
26. **26-close** (presenter). Next: the emergency fund, and what it's for.

## The Short

Scenes 01-coffee, 02-guess, 03-answer, 04-housing and 05-shape: the riddle of the coffee and
the size of the slices. About 45 seconds, one whole idea (eating out is about five dollars of
every hundred, housing about a third, so the big slices set the shape of a month), and it needs
nothing from the scenes left out. Same cut as the earlier episodes, the riddle.

None that stop it. The user asked for the next video of the playlist and for it to be uploaded
to YouTube and Instagram, so the brief did not go to them before the script. Decided here,
following what they decided for the earlier episodes and their wish not to be asked small
things:
