## 01-card

You put three thousand dollars on a credit card. Then you put the card away. And every month, you pay exactly what the bill asks for. The minimum. So here's the question. How long until you owe nothing?

## 02-guess

The card charges twenty-two percent a year. That's about the average in the United States. So take a guess. One year? Three? Five?

## 03-answer

The answer is about eleven years. And along the way, you pay back the three thousand, plus about thirty-eight hundred dollars in interest. The interest costs more than whatever you bought.

## 04-on-time

And you were never late. Not once. You did exactly what the bill asked. For eleven years.

## 05-rent

That's what a debt really costs. Not the price of the thing. The price of the time. Because a debt is rented money. And interest is the rent.

## 06-title
seconds: 3

## 07-apr

The rent comes as one number. The annual percentage rate. It says what a year of borrowing costs, for every hundred you owe. Interest and the lender's fees, rolled into one figure.

## 08-per-hundred

At twenty-two percent, a hundred dollars owed for a year costs twenty-two. Three thousand costs six hundred and sixty. Every year it stays there.

## 09-compare

And the same money has very different rents. In the United States, banks charge about seven and a half percent on a five-year car loan. About twelve on a personal loan. And over twenty-two on a credit card. One number, so you can put them side by side.

## 10-tub

So why eleven years? Picture a bathtub. The water in it is what you owe.

## 11-tap

Interest is a tap that never closes. And the fuller the tub, the faster it runs. On three thousand dollars, at twenty-two percent, it pours in fifty-five dollars a month.

## 12-bucket

Your payment is a bucket. Every month, you take some water out. What matters is how much more than the tap just put in.

## 13-minimum

Here is how most cards work out the minimum. This month's interest. Plus one percent of what you owe. So the first bill is eighty-five dollars. Fifty-five of it is interest. Only thirty comes off the debt.

## 14-shrinks

And here's the catch. As the debt goes down, the minimum goes down with it. The bill gets smaller every month. It feels like progress. But the level only drops one percent at a time.

## 15-five-years

After five years, and sixty payments, you still owe over sixteen hundred dollars. More than half.

## 16-time

None of this makes borrowing bad. It's how people get a home before they've saved for one. But in episode one, time worked for you. Here, time is the thing you pay for. And the minimum buys a lot of it.

## 17-question

So what would change the answer? Not a different card. Not a different rate. Just the size of the bucket.

## 18-fixed

Same debt. Same twenty-two percent. But this time, the payment doesn't shrink. It stays at eighty-five dollars, the very first bill, every month. Now it's paid off in under five years. And the interest is about nineteen hundred dollars. Half as much.

## 19-bigger

At a hundred and fifty a month, it's done in just over two years. And the interest is under eight hundred.

## 20-three-ways

Same debt. Same rate. Thirty-eight hundred, nineteen hundred, or eight hundred dollars. The only thing that changed is how long the water stayed in the tub.

## 21-in-full

And on most cards, there's one more case. Pay the whole balance by the due date, every month, and there's no interest on what you bought at all. The tap never opens.

## 22-common

None of this is rare. In the United States, about fifteen percent of cardholders pay only the minimum. And in twenty twenty-four, card interest added up to more than a hundred and sixty billion dollars.

## 23-persistent

In the United Kingdom, the regulator has a name for the far end of it. Persistent debt. Eighteen months of paying more in interest and charges than you repay of what you borrowed.

## 24-warning

And in the United States, card statements have to carry a warning. It says: if you make only the minimum payment each period, you will pay more in interest, and it will take you longer to pay off your balance. Now you know the arithmetic behind that sentence.

## 25-recap

Three things to keep. One: a debt is rented money, and the annual percentage rate is the rent. Two: what you pay depends on the rate, and on the time. Three: the minimum payment buys the most time. So it costs the most.
