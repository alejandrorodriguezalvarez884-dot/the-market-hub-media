Meta's third quarter, and a new kind of share.

Revenue was $64.6 billion in the three months to September 30, up 26% from $51.2 billion a year earlier. Ad impressions rose 10% and the average price per ad 14%. Family daily active people averaged 3.64 billion in September.

Operating income fell to $13.0 billion, from $20.5 billion, and the operating margin to 20%, from 40%. The quarter carries a $9.89 billion charge for Meta's agreement with 52 US state attorneys general over teen mental health and social media litigation; without it, Meta puts operating income at $22.9 billion. Net income was $9.9 billion, against $2.7 billion a year earlier, when the quarter had a much higher tax charge.

Capital spending, including finance lease payments, was $34.9 billion, and free cash flow was a negative $2.1 billion. Meta expects fourth-quarter revenue of $73 to $76 billion and 2026 capital spending of $135 to $145 billion.

The board also approved a new, non-voting Class C stock, effective January 19, 2027. Every Class A and Class B share will receive one Class C share as a one-time stock dividend, on dates still to be set. Meta expects the Class A price to reflect a two-for-one split afterwards, and says no shareholder's voting power changes. It gives capital raising, employee pay and possible acquisitions among the reasons for the new class.
