Chipmakers pulled Asia lower on Thursday, and Europe opened in the red.

Tokyo's Nikkei 225 closed 2.7% lower, at 62,907.84, and Seoul's Kospi 3.5% lower, at 5,533.77. It came a day after a tech-led rally on Wall Street, where the Nasdaq 100 closed 1.1% higher on Wednesday.

Chip and AI-linked names fell most. In Tokyo, Tokyo Electron lost 7%, SoftBank Group 6.9% and Advantest 6%, according to AP. In Seoul, SK hynix fell 5.5% and Samsung Electronics 4.0%. Foreign investors were net sellers of 113.2 billion won of Korean shares.

Local brokerages in Seoul pointed to profit-taking after Wednesday's chip-led rally, the Bank of Korea's tightening signal and renewed doubts about the cost of AI-related spending. The Bank of Korea held its base rate at 3.0%. AP linked the pressure on AI stocks to worries that data centre construction may slow as borrowing gets more expensive.

In Europe, the Euro Stoxx 50 was 1.5% lower and the FTSE 100 0.6% lower as of 07:35 UTC. ASML, ASM International, Infineon and Soitec fell between 3% and 5% in early trade, according to Investing.com.
